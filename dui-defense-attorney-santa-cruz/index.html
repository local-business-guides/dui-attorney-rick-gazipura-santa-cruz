How to tell if defense counsel fits your needs

Choosing a lawyer is not just about finding someone to take a call. You should be able to explain your concerns and understand the response. Ask how the attorney learns the facts, what records may help, and how they decide which issues to examine. A DUI Defense Attorney Santa Cruz should listen before drawing conclusions and speak clearly about what can and cannot be assessed at the first meeting.

You can ask how updates are handled and what would happen after the initial discussion. Rick Gazipura offers a free 30-minute telephone consultation to talk about your case and options. Use the conversation to see whether you can explain your situation comfortably and whether the attorney’s answers address the questions you brought.

Make a timeline before you talk

Your memory may be clearest when you write down the sequence of events in your own words. Keep it simple. Include what you recall happening before the encounter, during it, and afterward. If you’re unsure about a time or detail, mark it as uncertain instead of trying to make the account sound complete.

  • Record the approximate order of events and any times you know.
  • Note who was present, if that is relevant to your recollection.
  • List conversations or instructions you remember, using your own words.
  • Identify paperwork you received and the date shown on each item.
  • Separate what you personally remember from information you learned later.

This timeline is a starting point, not a legal conclusion. A DUI Defense Attorney Santa Cruz can compare your account with available records and decide what questions merit follow-up. Keep a copy for yourself so you can add details if more come to mind.

Preserve details without trying to solve the case

Gather documents connected with the arrest and keep them together. Don’t mark up originals or discard pages that seem unimportant. If you have notes, messages, or other records that may relate to the events, keep them intact and ask the attorney whether they are useful. You don’t need to interpret each item before seeking advice.

Avoid discussing the incident broadly online or asking others to shape your memory. It’s fine to note what you remember, but don’t treat another person’s account as your own. If you’ve already spoken with someone or shared information, mention that privately during your consultation. Honest context helps the attorney decide what information should be considered.

Questions to raise in an initial call

Bring the questions that affect your day-to-day life, as well as questions about the legal process. Ask what the attorney needs from you next, how the case review is likely to proceed, and how you can get clarification if something is confusing. A DUI Defense Attorney Santa Cruz can discuss an approach after learning the circumstances, but no lawyer should guarantee a specific outcome from a short conversation.
