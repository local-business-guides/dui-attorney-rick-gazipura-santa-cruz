First, get a clear picture of what happened

After an arrest, it’s easy to focus on what might happen next and miss useful details from the day itself. Write down what you remember while it’s fresh, including where you were stopped, what the officer said, and how the conversation and any tests unfolded. Don’t fill gaps with guesses. Your recollection is a starting point for a legal review, not a substitute for the documents in your case.

A short checklist before you call

Set aside the paperwork you received and make a simple folder, either on paper or on your phone. Before speaking with an attorney, try to have:

  • Any citation, court notice, or other papers related to the arrest
  • The date and general location of the stop
  • A written timeline of events, including what you remember before and after the stop
  • Questions about work, family responsibilities, driving, or other concerns
  • A note of any date printed on the paperwork that you don’t understand

Keep the original documents in a safe place. If something is missing, make a note of that rather than delaying a conversation until everything is collected.
